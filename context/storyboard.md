### Target versus absolute limit

For each **actual** speech interval D and stable voice rate R (1.8 / 2.3 / 2.7 words/sec):

- Absolute words = floor(D/1000 × R), absolute nonspace Unicode characters = floor(D/1000 × 16), exactly matching the unchanged validator.
- Preferred target = floor(absolute × 0.75), with 0/1 preserved for tiny discrete budgets. Clip it further to the capacity of max(0,D−200ms). A 2-word hard limit targets 1, 3 targets 2, 4 targets 3. Characters use the same independent margin. Targets never exceed absolute limits.
- The prompt supplies start/end, full duration, available speech duration after the pause, preferred word/character targets and absolute word/character maxima. It states: **The target is what you should normally use. The absolute maximum must never be exceeded.** Subdividing a window requires recalculating both values for every smaller utterance and another 200ms pause for each.
- Aggregate scene/video targets and maxima are sums of the per-window floors; dialogue and narration share them. Existing default 24% action/camera, 12% reaction, 4% transition and <=60% speech windows remain. Planning version is now 2; the persisted schema/prompt contract remains compatible with existing saved responses.

### Calculated plan supplied before dialogue

Pure `speech-budget.ts` computes a complete integer-ms scaffold from the actual selected duration. Default planning beats: one for 5s, two for 10s, five for 30s. Beats are not mandatory camera cuts: seamless continuous action/identity/product position can carry across boundaries without changing the user's concept. No story content is invented by this calculation.

For each actual scene interval of length D:

1. Reserve max(700ms, ceil(D × 24%)) at its start for character/product action and camera setup, capped to scene length. This honors the existing minimum action time even in a short returned scene.
2. Reserve ceil(D × 12%) for reaction/settling and ceil(D × 4%) for transition/ending at its end, each capped to remaining time after the action reserve. A very short corrected scene can have zero speech allowance: omit speech instead of inventing an impossible/zero-length utterance. Existing validators still reject zero-length actual speech.
3. The remaining middle window is available for dialogue/narration, at most 60% of the scene. Reserve a further 200ms per utterance for breath/pause.
4. For pace rate R (slow=1.8, natural=2.3, energetic=2.7), the prompt's **hard recommended ceiling** is floor((windowMs−200)/1000 × R) whitespace-separated words and floor((windowMs−200)/1000 × 16) nonspace Unicode characters. Clamp effective duration at zero; omit speech when its budget is zero. The existing validator's full-interval ceiling is also supplied, unchanged.
5. Whole-video word/character ceilings are the sum of the per-scene floored pause-aware ceilings. Dialogue and narration share them. Summing the floors prevents a scene from borrowing rounded allowances. Total speech-window duration and reserved non-speaking/pause milliseconds are supplied explicitly. Multiple utterances must subdivide the same window, subtract 200ms per utterance, and obey each smaller interval plus aggregate ceilings. They cannot reuse the scene's full allowance per line.

| Selected duration | Default beats | Total speech windows | Pause-aware speaking time | Natural max words | Natural max nonspace characters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5s | 1 × 5s | 3,000ms | 2,800ms | 6 | 44 |
| 10s | 2 × 5s | 6,000ms | 5,600ms | 12 | 88 |
| 30s | 5 × 6s | 18,000ms | 17,000ms | 35 | 270 |

These are computed results for those layouts, not fixed dialogue limits. Changing a scene/utterance interval or pace recalculates its own floor. E.g. a 2,000ms natural utterance has an unchanged validator maximum of 4 words/32 characters; its 200ms pause-aware recommendation is 4 words/28 characters. A 3,000ms default window recommends 6 words/44 characters. Silent scenes can use unused speech windows for longer realistic demonstration/camera work while retaining reaction/transition reserves; speech cannot occupy reserved action/reaction/transition time.

`prompt.ts` constructs the exact private context used by `provider.ts`: existing selected inputs/approved references plus `speechTimingPlan` (planningVersion=1, hardLimits=true), scene boundaries, all reserved intervals, speech start/end/duration/available time, rates, per-pace validator and recommended ceilings, and whole-video ceilings. Instructions require planning **duration → scene durations → action/camera time → speaking time → word/character budget → final dialogue**, concise 5/10s phrases, pauses, physically simple motion and no fast-voice workaround. The model still returns only the existing strict Storyboard schema, with no planning metadata fields. No second planning API call is made. No generated prose or private descriptors are added to logs.
